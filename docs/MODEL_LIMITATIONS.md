# Model limitations: what the shortfall forecaster can and cannot claim

Purpose: let any team member answer a hard question without bluffing. Each item says what it is, why it exists, and what we would do with more time. Numbers come from `oresight-backend/models/evaluation_report.json`, `model_metrics.json`, `oresight-backend/docs/simulator_sweep_2026-09-24.json` and the generator source, `scripts/synthetic_operations.py`.

## The one-paragraph honest summary

The forecaster beats both a train-mean and a persistence baseline on a time-ordered holdout (pooled RMSE 0.0566 vs 0.1168 and 0.0844; R² gain over persistence +0.302). It does so on a **synthetic production outcome whose causal structure we wrote ourselves**, so the result shows the pipeline can recover known effects, not that these effects hold at MOIL. The largest single caveat is item 2: most of its edge comes from a feature whose live source is also synthetic.

---

## 1. Bhandara loses to persistence in one rolling-origin window

- **What:** In the rolling-origin stability check, window 2 (2026-03-22 to 06-21), Bhandara's model RMSE is 0.0825 against persistence's 0.0768 (R² gain -0.071). The pooled model still wins that window (0.0610 vs 0.0669).
- **Why:** The model's edge is largest in monsoon periods, when rain and blast delays drive shortfall. In dry windows at a high-downtime site (Bhandara has 9 machines) yesterday's shortfall is already an excellent predictor, and the model has little extra to add.
- **What we did:** Recorded it, did not re-split, re-tune or re-frame. The rolling-origin windows are a stability check; the gate is the holdout, which passes at every site.
- **With more time:** More history than 21 months, per-site models or a site-level downtime interaction, and a dry-season-specific evaluation.

## 2. About 63% of the gain over persistence rides on `blast_delay_days_lag`, and its live source is synthetic

- **What:** Retraining without `blast_delay_days_lag` drops the pooled R² gain over persistence from +0.302 to +0.111 (RMSE 0.0566 to 0.0754); Nagpur falls from +0.259 to +0.050. (0.302 - 0.111) / 0.302 = 63%. The model still beats both baselines without it, so it is not a collapse, but the headline result depends on this one feature. It also carries the highest gain-based importance (0.389).
- **Why it is the biggest caveat:** The feature counts blast-delay days in the last week. Its live values come from the `blast_events` table, which is seeded from `data/blast_events.csv`: **synthetic delays generated by our own script, not real MOIL blast records.** In training, the same delays are what cause the losses (0.06 per delay-day for the next 5 days). So the model learned a relationship we planted, from an input we planted. With real MOIL data this feature could be weaker, absent, or differently timed, and the accuracy above would not carry over.
- **Consequence for the demo:** any statement that the model "predicts" blast-delay impact is a statement about the synthetic world.
- **With more time:** Replace the seed with MOIL's actual blast logs, retrain, and report the ablation again. Until then, do not present the +0.302 as an expected real-world figure.

## 3. Four groups of features have no causal term in the generator

- **What:** `rain_today_mm`, `soil_moisture_m3m3`, `days_since_last_maintenance`, and the day-of-week and month encodings. The generator gives none of them an effect on production, so whatever the model learned about them is a **dataset artifact and must never be cited as a finding.** Specifically: do not say the model learned that overdue maintenance raises shortfall, or that a weekday matters.
- **Why they are there:** They are physically plausible and would matter against real data. The `feature_provenance` block in `model_metrics.json` carries this warning inside the artifact.
- **Related:** `rain_today_mm` is trained on the observed same-day rain (a perfect forecast), and the generator's rain loss uses rain on t-1 and t-2 only. That is why the Simulator deliberately does not perturb it: "why doesn't today's rain move the forecast?" because in the world the model learned, it does not act on the same day.
- **Live gap:** Soil moisture is NaN in the live feature row because SMAP lags the rain record by about three days. The model never saw a NaN there in its training split. It is low importance (0.017) but is a departure from the training distribution, and the API reports it as a missing input.
- **With more time:** Give each a defensible mechanism and test it, or drop it.

## 4. `backlog_t` is too weak to learn reliably

- **What:** The generator's backlog effect is 0.02 x decayed backlog, about 1.79 percentage points of mean loss. The model recovers roughly 0.29 of the true slope (partial-dependence slope 0.0058 vs 0.02). The true effect across the 5th to 95th percentile of backlog is 0.031; the model's is 0.007.
- **Why:** The signal (sd 0.0098) is small next to the irreducible 3% noise, and backlog correlates with rain and blast delay.
- **With more time:** More data, or a design where the backlog effect is estimated separately. Do not present backlog as a learned driver.

## 5. The Stage 2 causal constants are documented assumptions, not MOIL measurements

- **What:** Every constant in the production generator is an ASSUMPTION marked as such in `scripts/synthetic_operations.py`: rain 0.004 and 0.002 per mm at lags 1 and 2, the 50 mm daily cap, the wet-pit rule (0.08 above 60 mm of 3-day rain), downtime class weights, backlog coefficient and decay, blast-delay probability and effect, 3% noise, and the 0.9 loss cap.
- **The measured-then-rescaled trail (reported at the time, not tuned silently):** The first pass used downtime weights of 0.30 / 0.20 / 0.15 / 0.25 / 0.10 and a backlog coefficient of 0.10. Two problems appeared: the weights summed to about 1.0 against a fleet ~14% down, giving 16.85 percentage points of loss from downtime alone, and the backlog feedback loop (gain 0.10 / 0.15 = 0.667) amplified all other losses about 3x. Together they gave a **60.6% mean loss**. Downtime weights were divided by 3 (to 0.100 / 0.067 / 0.050 / 0.083 / 0.033) and the backlog coefficient reduced to 0.02, giving a **13.4% mean loss** (rain 3.08pp, downtime 5.70pp, backlog 1.79pp, blast delay 2.87pp). Those two edits were plausibility choices to reach a believable loss level, not calibration to observed data.
- **What real rainfall does and does not do:** Rainfall is real, so the rain-to-loss relationship is the assumed coefficient applied to real weather.
- **With more time:** Fit these constants to MOIL shift records and downtime logs, and let the model be compared against that.

## 6. `blast_events` rows have NULL zone and placeholder yield

- **What:** The 67 seeded rows have `reserve_zone_id` NULL and `expected_yield_tonnes` set to one day of the site's target output on the planned date. Three further hand-entered demo rows (ids 29-31) exist and are not from the CSV.
- **Why:** The CSV delays are per site, not per zone; assigning a zone would invent a relationship. The schema requires a yield but the CSV has none, so a placeholder was used.
- **Consequence:** Nothing that reads blast yield or zone from these rows should be trusted. Only the delay days feed the model.
- **With more time:** Real blast plans with zones and yields.

## 7. Simulator limits

- **Severity is not a model input for two scenarios.** For `equipment_down` and `delay_blasting`, the severity control is only compared with the training range to raise an out-of-distribution flag. Only `rainfall_event` severity (mm of rain over 3 days) drives the projection. For the other two, the model input is set by the scenario itself (one more machine down; the delay in days).
- **Only the first condition is projected.** Stacking conditions in the UI changes the OOD checks but the forecast reflects the first condition only.
- **`heavy_rain_lag1` is used weakly.** In the Stage 3 grading its learned step is +0.029 against a true +0.248; the model relies on `rain_3d_mm` instead. So "60 mm in one day" and "60 mm over three days" give similar forecasts (means over the sampled states: +0.152 vs +0.120 shortfall), less different than the generator says they should be.
- **The state is not "now".** The starting point is the latest finished day with a complete observed rain record (usually 1-3 days old), shown in the results. Nothing is filled in for what is missing.
- **Delay durations outside 1-4 days are extrapolation.** Training delays lasted 1-4 days; longer requests are flagged out-of-distribution. The window also saturates at 7 days.
- **Reserve confidence and risk score are heuristics.** Reserve confidence decays by a fixed per-day rate; the risk score follows the model's predicted worsening (capped at 0.97). Neither is a learned model.
- **Planner ranking:** recommendation impact scores blend a fixed prior per option type with a bounded model signal, and the model term now saturates, so many options show the same +62%. That is a heuristic, not a measurement.

## 8. Sign was wired in; how much of the magnitude was learned? (Stage 5A diagnostic)

A skeptic can say the sweep result is correct by construction, because each scenario is wired to a feature with a real causal effect. The sign is indeed wired. To test the **magnitude**, we scaled each perturbation and compared the model's predicted shortfall change with the ground truth, over 26 dates x 3 sites (78 states), with no code changed:

| Scenario | Perturbation | Mean predicted shortfall change | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| equipment_down | +1 / +2 / +3 machines down | +0.048 / +0.088 / +0.117 | Doubling gives 1.86x, tripling 2.46x: scales, slightly sub-linear. Implied slope 0.28 / 0.26 / 0.23 per unit of fleet fraction, against a partial-dependence slope of 0.339 and a generator slope of 0.393 (Bhandara, with 9 machines, is steeper per unit). Direction and dose-response are learned; the size is about 25-35% low. |
| rainfall_event, 3 days | 20 mm vs 60 mm | +0.034 vs +0.120 | 3.55x for 3x the rain: scales, mildly super-linear (the wet-pit and 3-day features). About 0.0017-0.0020 per mm of 3-day rain, in line with the model's 0.00197 per mm partial-dependence slope and with the generator (0.004 at lag 1, 0.002 at lag 2, so about 0.002 per mm spread over three days). The 0.0044 per mm figure is per mm of rain on the single previous day, a different quantity. |
| delay_blasting | 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 days | +0.029 / +0.077 / +0.135 / +0.174 (added lag days 0.9 / 1.8 / 2.6 / 3.5) | Per added delay-day 0.033 / 0.043 / 0.051 / 0.050, against a 0.055 partial-dependence slope and a 0.0446 empirical slope (the feature counts 7 days while the true effect lasts 5). One day is under-predicted; from three days the slope is near truth. |

**Conclusion:** nothing flatlines. The sweep measures learned dose-response, not just plumbing: the sign is wired, and the magnitude scales with the perturbation and lands within roughly 25-40% of the generator's ground truth (closest for rain and multi-day blast delays, lowest for equipment). Two caveats stay attached: the truth here is our own generator, and the model is only as good as that world (items 2 and 5).

## 9. The old-model comparison is indicative, not exact

- The recommended comparison is in `oresight-backend/docs/simulator_sweep_2026-09-24.json`: over the same 265 days (2026-01-01 to 09-22), the current model lowers the forecast on 244-264 days in every site x scenario cell. The previous model is majority-correct in only 3-4 of 9 cells depending on the replay method, and it *raised* the forecast on most days for blast delays.
- **Caveat printed with the old-model column:** the previous simulator had no notion of an as-of date; it read `datetime.now()` and the current database state. Replaying it is therefore a method choice. We ran two (varying only calendar features, and faking the whole instant); the conclusion is the same under both, but the individual old-model numbers differ between them and depend on the database state at measurement time. An earlier recorded baseline could not be reproduced by either method, so it is not used anywhere.

## 10. Other things worth knowing

- **Perfect-forecast ceiling:** `rain_today_mm` is trained on observed rain, so accuracy is an optimistic ceiling relative to live use with a real forecast.
- **Regional weather:** rainfall is sampled at about 9-13 km resolution, not in-pit.
- **Weather licensing:** Open-Meteo's public API is non-commercial; production use needs a commercial plan or a self-hosted instance.
- **Watcher rain events are model forecasts,** not gauge readings, and an advisory coexists with a heavy event for the same storm (each auto-resolves on its own threshold).
- **Environment:** the model is only valid under the xgboost it was trained with (2.1.4); the loader refuses a mismatch.
